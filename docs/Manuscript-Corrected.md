## 1 Introduction

This chapter presented the background of the study which established the context of the research. This section explained why this particular research was important and essential to understanding the main aspect of the study.

### 1.1 Project Context

The management of government records has become increasingly critical as public offices handle growing volumes of data across various programs and services. Governments, healthcare systems, and social institutions face growing demands to improve their record management systems as public institutions expand their operational duties [1]. Countries are adopting information and communication technology (ICT) as their main approach to update public service operations, which includes handling government records and beneficiary information. The digital age requires public sector organizations to manage their electronic records effectively because Electronic Records Management Systems (ERMS) have become essential for improving data processing, regulatory compliance, and service delivery efficiency [2].

Policymakers and researchers across Southeast Asia face urgent challenges because local government offices fail to adopt modern recordkeeping systems [3]. Studies have found that archival practices in many local governments remain manual, fragmented, and dependent on informal staff knowledge, which causes operational inefficiencies and data protection deficiencies. The absence of a centralized or digital archive registry results in records that are hard to locate, often damaged, and at risk of permanent loss, directly affecting service delivery and internal administration. Researchers and government agencies throughout the region have started to recommend technology-based solutions for records administration because of the persistent challenges which organizations face [4].

Local government offices in the Philippines require better and more dependable systems to handle public records and safeguard their stored data. The Philippine local government units (LGUs) face operational challenges because they lack both trained staff and established record management procedures. Local governments need to develop effective systems for recordkeeping and data protection because they increasingly adopt digital technologies to deliver services to their communities. The offices continue to use outdated systems which rely on paper documents and spreadsheet software for their operations. The outdated procedures create administrative hold-ups which prevent essential services from reaching the communities that require them [5] [6]. A government official has noted that effective record management is the key to the successful implementation of public service programs, stressing that if information is scattered and obtained manually, service delivery will inevitably fail [6].

The Philippine government has enacted multiple laws and regulations to achieve both good governance and efficient public service delivery. Republic Act No. 9470, known as the National Archives of the Philippines Act of 2007, mandates that all public records with enduring value held by government offices, including all branches of government, constitutional offices, and local government units, be properly managed and preserved [7]. According to the law, public records function as essential tools that enable government operations to run effectively, while they also serve as vital historical documents that protect national heritage and cultural identity [7]. The National Archives of the Philippines issued Memorandum Circular No. 2104-01, which contains the Electronic Records Management Policy (ERMP), on April 21, 2021, to mandate all government agencies to develop their Electronic Records Management System (ERMS) according to established guidelines for transitioning from physical documents to digital document storage [8].

The increasing size of local government activities at provincial boundaries demonstrates the necessity of dependable record management systems. The Philippine Statistics Authority reports that the province's total population reached 845,066 by 2024 and has been experiencing continuous population growth throughout the years [9]. The rising number of residents and registered beneficiaries creates greater pressure on local government units to manage their recordkeeping processes while maintaining precise records. Research assessment of local government unit records management systems shows that security, accessibility, and retrieval operations remain poorly managed because most offices lack sufficient staff capabilities and monitoring systems, and they do not have established security protocols which are required to conduct regular security assessments [10].

The research study used the daily operational difficulties that local government units face as its main foundation to determine its research objectives. The first objective of the project required the team to determine which data the proposed system needed to collect because OSCA in Bulan, Sorsogon lacked a dependable system for data organization and retrieval. The team needed to conduct a complete assessment of current data collection methods, beneficiary identification processes, and office operations because the developed system required actual operational requirements instead of a standard solution.

The Office for Senior Citizens Affairs (OSCA) in Bulan, Sorsogon functions like other local government offices because it uses paper documents and spreadsheet software for its operations. Research has shown that using an ERMS to solve the difficulties of manual record maintenance will establish a centralized system which enables efficient record collection and storage together with record processing, which will lead to better organizational record management practices [11]. The research team needed to identify the required features for an enhanced system because they discovered existing system restrictions. The staff needed a digital registration module which would enable them to enter and protect each beneficiary's information and documents because lost or unreadable records had become a major issue.

The Office for Senior Citizens Affairs (OSCA) in Bulan, Sorsogon, Philippines, needed this project because it encountered real recordkeeping problems that required solutions. A properly constructed records management system delivers combined systems which enable instant data tracking, work handling, user enrollment, document creation, and system configuration control to prove system performance, security, data handling capabilities, and operational dependability [11]. These inefficiencies are particularly consequential given the range of benefit programs the Office for Senior Citizens Affairs (OSCA) is responsible for administering on behalf of the senior citizen population of Bulan.

The Office for Senior Citizens Affairs (OSCA) benefit programs cover both local and national programs, which require different eligibility criteria and payment methods that need exact and updated documentation. The Social Pension program at the national level delivers a quarterly cash benefit of ₱3,000.00 to indigent senior citizens who are at least 60 years old. The national government awards one-time cash gifts to seniors who reach important age milestones through the Octogenarian Grant of ₱10,000.00 upon reaching ages 80 and 85, the Nonagenarian Grant of ₱10,000.00 upon reaching ages 90 and 95, and the Centenarian Award of ₱100,000.00 upon reaching 100 according to the Centenarians Act of 2016. The age-milestone grants provide benefits to all eligible seniors who meet the age criteria. The mayor's office runs the Precentenarian Program at the local level to provide funding for seniors who are between 85 and 99 years old when funds are available. The governor's office provides a ₱5,000.00 grant through the Provincial Program to seniors who are between 80 and 99 years old without considering their financial condition. The Office for Senior Citizens Affairs (OSCA) needs to keep a complete record system that enables quick access to all beneficiary information due to the multiple programs that require different age limits, funding methods, and payment times.

The Office for Senior Citizens Affairs (OSCA) of Bulan, Sorsogon faces a major recordkeeping problem, which results from administering benefit programs through its current manual system. Each program operates under its own set of rules: the Social Pension requires the office to maintain and verify the indigency status of every enrolled beneficiary on an ongoing basis, since only those classified as indigent are entitled to receive it. The age-milestone grants, namely the Octogenarian, Nonagenarian, and Centenarian programs, require the office to track the exact birth dates of all registered seniors and proactively identify those who are approaching or have reached the qualifying ages of 80, 90, and 100, respectively, so that no beneficiary is overlooked when they become eligible. The local programs, namely the Precentenarian Program and the Provincial Program, add another layer of complexity because they are tied to conditional fund releases rather than fixed schedules, requiring the office to maintain a ready and accurate list of qualified beneficiaries at all times so that benefits can be released immediately once funding arrives. The use of paper records and spreadsheets to manage all programs at the same time creates a high risk that some beneficiaries will be missed, that outdated information will lead to incorrect assessments, or that delays in updating records will cause eligible seniors to miss a payout cycle they are legally entitled to receive.

The registration process itself further compounds the recordkeeping burden of the office. A person becomes eligible to register with the Office for Senior Citizens Affairs (OSCA) on the very day they turn 60 years old. The registration process requires three months, which begins after the applicant registers, because the office needs to finish document verification, eligibility evaluation, and coordination with the funding agency. The office needs to handle three different groups of people: new applicants whose processing status varies, current beneficiaries who participate in multiple benefit programs, and senior citizens who will become eligible for additional grants when they reach higher age brackets. The manual system needs numerous workers to track these transitions, and this process leads to frequent mistakes. A senior citizen who has already been receiving the Social Pension, for example, may simultaneously become eligible for the Octogenarian Grant upon turning 80 and eventually for the Precentenarian and Provincial programs as well—transitions that require prompt action from the office but are easily overlooked without a system that automatically flags such changes. The Office for Senior Citizens Affairs (OSCA) requires a digital management system to operate effectively because the system provides essential support, which enables the office to complete its responsibility of delivering complete benefits to every senior citizen in Bulan at the correct time.

The research study found that organizations which deploy ERMS to replace their manual recordkeeping process achieve better data management through automated record collection and processing, which leads to improved operational performance [11]. The system must contain integrated components which handle beneficiary registration, benefit tracking, report creation, and data protection to solve the operational deficiencies found in the existing OSCA system [11]. The researchers developed a web-based system for managing senior citizen records and benefits at the Office for Senior Citizens Affairs (OSCA) of Bulan, Sorsogon. The system digitalizes the entire process of managing senior citizen records, which enables automatic tracking of benefit eligibility for every program and decreases the workload of the OSCA staff while increasing the quality, accuracy, and timeliness of the services delivered to the senior citizens of the municipality.

### 1.2 Purpose and Description

The Local Government Unit of Bulan operates its Office of Senior Citizen Affairs through a dedicated team which follows proven methods to deliver benefits to the community, so they have earned the public's trust and recognition. The office uses paper documents together with unorganized files, which leads to time-consuming data retrieval operations that need between 30 minutes and over an hour to complete. The establishment of a centralized database system enables the Office for Senior Citizens Affairs (OSCA) staff and the presidents of the Barangay Senior Citizens Associations (BSCA) to efficiently handle tasks which involve registering senior citizens, updating their records, tracking their benefits, and generating reports. The office faces multiple challenges, yet it can achieve substantial benefits by implementing a web-based system that enhances operational efficiency while decreasing mistakes, uses data analytics to improve decision-making and program development, and establishes improved connections between the municipal office and the barangays.

The purpose of this system is to automate essential functions of the Office for Senior Citizens Affairs (OSCA) operations. The Senior Citizen Registration module enables the BSCA Presidents of each barangay to encode the personal information, privacy consent, and supporting documents (valid ID, birth certificate, and photo) of senior citizens, one at a time or through a bulk upload of a CSV or Excel file, eliminating the reliance on manual recordkeeping. The system assigns each registered senior an OSCA ID number, accepts only applicants who are at least 60 years old, and blocks a record with the same name and birthdate as an existing or archived record. The Eligibility Verification feature lets the OSCA Admin and the OSCA Head approve a pending registration, mark it not eligible with a reason, or return it to the President when its documents need to be corrected. Through benefit tracking, the system lists the qualified seniors of each scheduled release batch, records whether each senior received the benefit, keeps the proof of release, and blocks paying the same benefit twice for the same period. It also handles the benefit requests that the Presidents file for individual seniors, which the Head verifies and releases. The Reports and Analytics module enables OSCA personnel to create precise and timely reports from the stored records. The OSCA Admin generates a Municipal Summary, a Barangay Summary, or a Benefit Distribution report, and the OSCA Head reviews it, returns it for revision when needed, approves it, and publishes it. The figures of each report are saved at the time it is generated, so the report that the Head approves is the same report that is published, printed, and exported. The Notifications feature delivers in-app notifications to every user and, when the Admin turns them on, email advisories to the BSCA Presidents about registration decisions, document corrections, benefit release schedules, milestone grants, announcements, and published reports. The Age Threshold Detection feature checks every day which active seniors have turned 80, 85, 90, 95, or 100 and have not yet received the matching grant, which keeps beneficiary records complete and accurate over time.

This study is significant to the following beneficiaries:

**To the OSCA Staff.** This study provides a web-based system that enables the staff to effectively and efficiently verify registrations, update personal details, track the distribution of benefits, generate reports, and view accurate analytics.

**To the BSCA Presidents.** This study gives the Barangay Senior Citizens Association presidents a single place to register the senior citizens of their barangay, follow up on their registrations and benefit requests, and record the release of benefits.

**To the Senior Citizens.** The study helps ensure that they receive their benefits correctly and on time, hence facilitating their access to the required services and benefits.

**To the Local Government of Bulan.** The study aims to contribute to better data management and reporting for improved planning and decision-making for senior citizens' welfare programs.

**To the Researchers.** The study gives the researchers hands-on experience building a web-based information system, applying academic knowledge in software development, database management, and analytics to solve real problems faced by the Office of Senior Citizen Affairs of LGU-Bulan.

**To the Future Researchers.** This study can serve as a reference for future researchers who will develop or extend systems that help OSCA offices register senior citizens, update their personal information, monitor benefit distribution, and generate reports in a faster and more organized way.

### 1.3 Objectives of the Study

The general objective of this study is to develop a web-based Office of Senior Citizen Affairs Assistant Management System for LGU-Bulan. Specifically, it aims:

1. To determine the information requirements of the proposed system, including senior citizen profiling, beneficiary qualification, and the existing OSCA workflow.
2. To implement the following features:
    1. Senior Citizen Registration
    2. Eligibility Verification
    3. Benefit Tracking
    4. Reports
    5. Roles and Access Control

    3. Performance efficiency; and
    4. Security.

### 1.4 Scope and Limitation

This study was conducted to develop an Office of Senior Citizen Affairs Assistant Management System. The system is intended to enhance the management of senior citizen information and assist in the monitoring of benefits delivered by the LGU. By accomplishing this objective, errors and duplication of records are avoided, and the management of senior citizen information is improved within the municipality of Bulan, Sorsogon.

The study first established the information requirements needed to develop the proposed system. The research team conducted a one-on-one interview with the OSCA Head of LGU-Bulan, Sorsogon, to examine three essential components: the information about senior citizens that needs to be profiled, the qualification criteria for each benefit program, and the current operations of the OSCA office. The system stores the personal information of registered senior citizens, which includes their name, birthdate, age, sex, civil status, barangay, address, contact details, association membership, data privacy consent, and supporting documents. It also records how benefits are distributed and tracks the release of each benefit.

The system uses role-based access control with three user roles to protect data. BSCA Presidents (Barangay Leaders) register the senior citizens of their own barangay and can view only those records. Any change they make to a registered record is submitted as an edit request that the OSCA Head must approve. They also correct and resubmit documents when a registration is returned to them, record the death of a senior together with the death certificate, request benefits for approved seniors, record whether each senior in a release batch received the benefit, and upload proof of release such as the signed payroll and photos. The OSCA Head verifies the eligibility of new registrations, approves or rejects edit requests, verifies, rejects, and releases benefit requests, schedules benefit release batches, posts announcements, monitors data and analytics, and reviews, approves, and publishes reports. The OSCA Admin also verifies eligibility, maintains all senior citizen records across barangays (update, mark inactive, archive, restore, and delete), updates benefit release statuses, completes release batches, manages user accounts and the permissions of each role, turns email advisories on or off, reviews the audit logs, backs up and restores the database, and generates reports for the Head's approval. The Admin can turn individual permissions on or off for the Head and the BSCA Presidents, such as approving registrations, scheduling releases, registering seniors, requesting benefits, and exporting reports. Senior citizens do not log in to the system; their records are encoded by the BSCA Presidents and maintained on their behalf by the OSCA Admin and the OSCA Head.

Second is identifying the system requirements, which help address the challenges associated with managing senior citizen profiles in Bulan, Sorsogon. The system requirements that emerged from Objective 1 led to seven system features: senior citizen registration, eligibility verification, benefit tracking, reports, user roles and access control with three access levels, notifications, and age threshold detection. Notifications are delivered inside the system to every user and, when enabled by the OSCA Admin, as email to the BSCA Presidents. The system also includes announcements posted by the Head, which the Presidents can comment on, and direct messages between the Head and the Presidents. The system, however, is also constrained in several areas. The system does not send SMS. Senior citizens do not have accounts and receive no notifications directly; advisories reach them through their BSCA President. The physical ID card creation for senior citizens was not implemented due to technical and time constraints. The system generates three types of reports: a Municipal Summary, which presents registrations and benefit releases per barangay together with the totals for the municipality of Bulan; a Barangay Summary, which presents the same figures and the list of registered senior citizens for one barangay; and a Benefit Distribution report, which lists the benefits each senior citizen has received and those still pending release. Only the OSCA Admin can generate a report. The OSCA Head reviews each report and either approves it or returns it to the Admin with remarks for revision, and only an approved report can be published. Once published, a report can no longer be changed and is made available to the BSCA Presidents concerned: a President can view the published reports for their own barangay and the Municipal Summary, which contains totals only and no personal information of senior citizens. Reports can be viewed on screen, printed, and exported to PDF, Word, or Excel, subject to the export permission that the Admin sets for each role.

The OSCA staff of LGU-Bulan need descriptive analytics because it enables them to understand their data more effectively. The office selected descriptive analytics because it provides an overview of past and present data, which enables users to understand their existing operational status. OSCA requires descriptive analytics because it delivers information through simple displays that people can easily understand, while predictive and prescriptive analytics demand advanced modeling procedures and extensive data collections. The OSCA staff can access summarized records through visual displays which include graphs and tables that show the total registered senior citizens, the age distribution, the benefit distribution across barangays, and additional barangay and municipal data. The BSCA Presidents see the same analytics limited to their own barangay. Descriptive analytics provides OSCA personnel with visual data displays which show summarized content that enables them to detect patterns and oversee benefit distribution while making decisions that require no advanced technical skills or statistical knowledge.

Finally, the study evaluated the software quality of the developed system based on the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality model. The evaluation was carried out by testing and assessing the system against four quality characteristics. Functional suitability, which determines if the system delivers the necessary capabilities to achieve OSCA's goals, was evaluated through black-box testing with Postman and through automated feature tests. The testing used structured test cases to check if the system functions produced correct results when given specific inputs. The evaluation of reliability, which tests the system's capacity to provide consistent operation without unplanned shutdowns, was conducted through Apache JMeter testing. The test used multiple simulated users to determine how the system responded to increased demands without experiencing system failures or performance drops. The performance efficiency assessment measured the system's capacity to complete tasks within acceptable time limits while utilizing system resources efficiently. Google Lighthouse and GTmetrix were used to assess website performance through their analysis of page load times, response times, and resource usage during typical operational situations. The security assessment measured the system's effectiveness in safeguarding data from unauthorized users while permitting only authorized staff to view confidential information. The assessment used OWASP Zed Attack Proxy (ZAP), a free security testing tool that conducts automatic security checks to discover major web application threats, including SQL injection, cross-site scripting, and unauthorized access attempts. The evaluation focuses on these four quality characteristics and does not include other ISO/IEC 25010 attributes such as maintainability, portability, and usability. The results apply to the specific operational environment of LGU-Bulan because the study focused exclusively on it.

The research set boundaries which show the future direction of the Office of Senior Citizen Affairs Assistant Management System. The research team established particular objectives which helped them develop a system that fulfilled the practical requirements of the office within the available research resources and time limits. The study acknowledges its limitations, which include the system's inability to work offline and its restriction to descriptive analytics functions. The system has a responsive, user-friendly design which enables access on Android devices, iOS devices, tablets, and desktop computers, and on mobile devices it can be installed to the home screen from the browser. The system requires continuous internet access because it cannot operate without an active internet connection. For security, a signed-in session ends automatically after 30 minutes without activity. The system underwent testing on Android and iOS devices to confirm its ability to function across different platforms while maintaining performance in all operating systems.

The proponents of this study consider the current system, despite its limitations, to be a substantial advancement which enables OSCA to modernize its operations while delivering better service to the senior citizens of LGU-Bulan. Future studies may build upon this work by addressing the identified limitations and expanding the system's capabilities to serve a wider range of users and local government units.

## 2 Review of Related Systems

This section presents a review of multiple sources that are relevant to the research objectives established by the study. The review includes three main components: information needs assessment, system attribute evaluation, and user experience testing.

### 2.1 Digital Systems for Senior Citizen Services

Digital systems have been used to enhance service delivery to elderly people through better accessibility features, health assessment systems, and social interaction solutions. The APEC Digital Solutions and Technologies for Senior Citizens' Connectivity and Healthiness report presents various digital platforms that aim to improve the well-being of the elderly through telehealth systems, mobile apps, and online service portals [12]. These systems enable better healthcare delivery, effective communication, and social interaction among users. The document emphasizes policy and implementation matters while showing less interest in technical details about system components and the programming languages used.

Similarly, the study of Zhang et al. [13] on the role of digital technologies in improving the user experience of elderly users gave much emphasis to usability, accessibility, and human-computer interaction. The importance of designing user-friendly interfaces for senior citizens has been emphasized in this study. Though it gives an idea regarding system design principles, it does not clearly mention the programming languages used in system development, as much emphasis was given to user experience.

On the other hand, a more technical approach was provided by the study in [14]. In this study, the author discussed different smart systems for elderly care using digital technology, such as IoT-based systems. In most of these systems, technologies like Python, IoT tools, cloud database systems, and web-based tools are used for monitoring and data processing. More emphasis was placed on data analytics and automation in elderly care services, particularly in health monitoring systems.

In comparison, all three studies emphasize the importance of digital technologies in improving senior citizen services. The APEC report in [12] focuses on policy and large-scale implementation, the study of Zhang et al. in [13] emphasizes usability and user experience, while the MDPI study in [14] highlights technical and data-driven solutions using modern programming tools. Despite their contributions, these studies are more focused on healthcare, accessibility, and user interaction, rather than administrative systems such as profiling and benefit monitoring.

The primary gap that needs to be addressed exists because local governments lack systems which combine three functions: profiling, benefit monitoring, and descriptive analytics. The research studies demonstrate how digital technologies can create benefits for organizations, yet they fail to provide a solution which enables centralized management of senior citizen records and tracking of government benefits.

The MDPI study in [14] uses Python, IoT, and cloud-based platforms to show modern system development, while the studies in [12] [13] work with conceptual frameworks which lack specific technology details. This shows a research gap in system development for administrative service work.

The proposed system addresses these limitations by utilizing PHP (Laravel), MySQL, and React, which are suitable for developing a web-based administrative system. The proposed system concentrates on profiling, benefit monitoring, and descriptive analytics, which better meets the operational requirements of the Office of Senior Citizen Affairs (OSCA) in LGU-Bulan than the existing studies.

### 2.2 Web-Based Profiling and Information Management

Web-based profiling systems play a significant role in improving data organization, accessibility, and efficiency in managing large volumes of user information. These systems are widely adopted in government and institutional settings to replace manual record-keeping processes.

A project related to this is the study in [15], which developed a computerized system that automates the registration of senior citizens in Malita, Davao Occidental. The study found that the system made data entry, retrieval, and report generation faster and more efficient. In terms of technology, it utilized PHP, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and MySQL [15]. However, despite its functionality in profiling senior citizens, it was still limited in terms of incorporating other features such as analytics, monitoring, and tracking of benefits.

The study Development of a Web-based Records Management System in [16] created an Electronic Records Management System (ERMS) for the Office of Senior Citizen Affairs (OSCA) in the Philippines, which needed to centralize its records and make its data accessible while improving its operational processes through automated records management. The system contained four primary functions: member registration, record classification, report generation, and online monitoring. The development team used Laravel and MySQL to implement the system. The system was tested against the ISO/IEC 25010 standard, which showed that its usability, reliability, and security had improved.

Both web-based platforms show how their systems effectively enhance data management operations. The first system in [15] focuses on basic profiling and automation, while the second system in [16] emphasizes centralized records management using a modern framework. The second system is more advanced in terms of system architecture and evaluation, whereas the first is simpler and primarily designed for registration and profiling.

The existing research contains important findings, yet there are remaining research gaps. The system in [15] lacks analytics, monitoring capabilities, and scalability, while the system in [16], although more advanced, focuses mainly on records management rather than benefit tracking and descriptive analytics. The two systems do not provide a unified platform which allows users to manage citizen registration and service evaluation through a dedicated system for senior citizen services.

### 2.3 Benefit Monitoring Systems in Local Government Units

Benefit monitoring systems are essential to maintain transparency and accountability while delivering public services in an effective manner. Local government units use these systems to monitor their beneficiaries and assess their service delivery performance while making data-based decisions.

The framework in [17] provides a structured, phase-based guide for monitoring and evaluating social innovations in health. It defines social innovation in health as "inclusive solutions to address the health care delivery gap that meet the needs of end users through a multi-stakeholder, community-engaged process." The framework consists of seven iterative phases derived from Nesta's seven stages of innovation: (1) Opportunities and Challenges, (2) Generating Ideas, (3) Developing and Testing, (4) Making the Case, (5) Delivering and Implementing, (6) Growing and Scaling, and (7) Changing Systems. Every phase includes particular research methods together with free resources which assist implementers, researchers, and community members in their work.

The framework operates independently from specific programming languages or software systems. It serves as a conceptual and methodological instrument that can be utilized in various environments. It explains which research methods should be utilized during each phase, including scoping reviews, randomized controlled trials, social impact assessments, discrete choice experiments, and qualitative methods such as ethnographic study and grounded theory. The framework also examines how hackathons and crowdsourcing activities create social innovations while explaining the need for end-user co-creation throughout all development stages.

The system in [18] was developed through the Agile System Development Life Cycle (SDLC) model and was tested against the requirements of the ISO/IEC 25010 Software Product Quality Standard across eight quality characteristics: functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, usability, reliability, security, maintainability, and portability. The development process used PHP for server-side programming, MariaDB for database management, and XAMPP for local server operation. The selected tools are accessible and match the existing government office infrastructure in the Philippines, and they enable local governments to implement solutions without needing costly enterprise-level systems.

The research study of Antwi-Boasiako et al. in [19] does not study software systems or programming languages. It uses qualitative research methods to conduct interviews, analyze documents, and observe field activities within decentralized local government planning systems. The study presents a conceptual framework which describes four sets of factors that work together to determine how decentralized systems function: (1) sociopolitical context, (2) organizational factors at the Metropolitan, Municipal, and District Assembly (MMDA) level, (3) policy-level factors, and (4) human resource factors.

The study shows that decentralized monitoring system implementation needs sufficient resources and proper institutional setup to achieve better planning results, increased service accountability, better service quality, and decreased poverty levels. The study found multiple major restrictions, which included inadequate training and capacity of personnel, the lack of effective accountability systems, the unavailability of financial resources, the weakness of data management systems, and the impact of political forces. The study is especially important for the proposed system because it shows why local government information systems fail or perform poorly and which institutional factors need to exist for successful long-term monitoring.

The study by Vasquez et al. in [18] serves as the nearest equivalent to the proposed system because it covers senior citizen demographic information, pension distribution, and birthday records, yet it does not provide a complete benefit monitoring function. The system does not have a specialized module for tracking benefits that would document which benefits senior citizens received, their date of receipt, and any outstanding benefits they have yet to receive. The proposed LGU-Bulan system explicitly addresses this gap through a Benefit Tracking feature that records, for each senior citizen, the release status of the Social Pension, the Octogenarian, Nonagenarian, and Centenarian grants, the Precentenarian Program, and the Provincial Program, together with the release date, the period, and the proof of release, enabling real-time monitoring of benefit distribution status at both the barangay and municipal levels.

Three of the studies examined in this research focus on areas outside the boundaries of Sorsogon province. The framework in [17] draws on Nigeria, China, India, and Bangladesh. The study described in [20] focuses on public health facilities in another region of the Philippines with its own demographic and institutional makeup. The decentralized monitoring study in [19] is set in Ghana. The researchers developed this system as a solution which meets the specific requirements of OSCA in Bulan, Sorsogon, where the local office cannot keep up with the growing service demands of a province of 845,066 residents and its increasing number of senior citizens through manual record-keeping methods. The OSCA-Bulan system requires local knowledge because its operations depend on the staffing and data handling methods of the office and its barangays.

The proposed system provides an advanced solution through a complete system which combines senior citizen profiling with benefit monitoring and descriptive analytics functions. It provides an operational solution which the Office of Senior Citizen Affairs (OSCA) in LGU-Bulan needs, while the reviewed studies mostly present theoretical frameworks and concepts.

### 2.4 Descriptive Analytics in Public Service

Descriptive analytics plays an important role in improving public service systems by transforming raw data into meaningful insights through dashboards and visualization tools. In the study of Cortez et al. in [20], descriptive analytics was used to analyze the implementation of health information systems in public health facilities. The researchers applied dashboarding, analysis, and reporting techniques to visualize operational data such as reporting platforms, trained personnel, and facility capabilities.

Through the use of digital reporting systems and visualization dashboards, administrators were able to easily interpret collected data and identify gaps in system implementation. The study highlights the importance of data analytics tools and database systems in supporting evidence-based decision-making within government institutions [20].

Both studies used descriptive analytics and visualization dashboards to support monitoring and decision-making in public service systems. The study in [20] focused on analyzing the use of health information systems in public health facilities through dashboarding, analysis, and reporting of operational data. Meanwhile, the study in [21] concentrated on monitoring institutional programs and activities using interactive dashboards and visual reports. Although both studies utilized descriptive analytics to summarize and interpret data, they differ in their application. The study of Cortez et al. in [20] emphasized evaluating health information system usage in government facilities, while the study of De Jesus et al. in [21] focused on performance monitoring of organizational programs and activities.

The two systems provide the basic functions of monitoring operations and displaying data, but they lack the ability to handle records of beneficiaries and monitor government benefits. Neither study establishes a single system that combines profiling with eligibility checking and benefit tracking, and neither addresses the problems that local government offices face with paper records and with keeping track of the people who receive benefits. Local government offices often experience difficulties when they need to handle paper documents while monitoring the benefits that senior citizens receive. The current manual process does not function effectively because it lacks an efficient method for organizing senior citizen benefits. A system is needed that supports decision-making through the analysis of the data the office holds on senior citizens and their benefits, combining senior citizen profiling, benefit monitoring, and analysis to enable data-based decisions on benefit distribution.

The reviewed studies emphasize the use of web-based systems, databases, and data visualization tools for implementing descriptive analytics dashboards. ### 3.3 Technical Terms

The following are the conceptual and operational definitions of terms used in the study:

**Age Threshold Detection.** The automatic identification of persons who reach a qualifying age. In this study, it refers to the daily check that identifies active senior citizens who have reached 80, 85, 90, 95, or 100 and have not yet received the matching grant, and notifies the users concerned.

**Archive.** The removal of a record from the active list while keeping it in storage. In this study, it refers to senior citizen records that are no longer listed but can be restored by the OSCA Admin.

**Audit Log.** A record of the actions performed in a system. In this study, it refers to the record of who created, changed, approved, released, or deleted data in the system, kept for accountability.

**Benefit Monitoring.** The process of tracking and overseeing the distribution of privileges and services granted to qualified individuals. In this study, it refers to the feature of the system that records and monitors the benefits received by each registered senior citizen in LGU-Bulan.

**Benefit Request.** A request for a benefit for a specific person. In this study, it refers to a request filed by a BSCA President for a benefit for one approved senior citizen, which the OSCA Head verifies and then releases.

**BSCA President.** The president of the Barangay Senior Citizens Association. In this study, it refers to the barangay-level user of the system who registers the senior citizens of the barangay and records their benefit releases. The BSCA President is also called the Barangay Leader.

**Descriptive Analytics.** A method of analyzing existing data to identify patterns, trends, and insights through visual representations. In this study, it refers to the analytics features of the system that generate charts, graphs, and statistical summaries to help OSCA personnel better understand the senior citizen population and their benefit utilization.

**Eligibility Review.** The checking of whether an applicant meets the requirements of a program. In this study, it refers to the page where the OSCA Admin and the OSCA Head approve a pending registration, mark it not eligible, or return it for document correction.

**Office of Senior Citizen Affairs Assistant Management System.** The web-based system developed in this study for the OSCA of LGU-Bulan to register senior citizens, verify their eligibility, track their benefits, generate reports, and provide descriptive analytics.

**Profiling.** The process of collecting and organizing relevant personal information about an individual in a structured manner. In this study, it refers to the systematic recording of each senior citizen's personal details, such as name, age, address, and contact number, within the system.

**Release Batch.** A scheduled distribution of one benefit program for a period to one or more barangays. In this study, the system lists the qualified senior citizens of each batch automatically, and the release status of each one is recorded.

**Senior Citizen.** A person who is sixty (60) years old and above, as defined under Republic Act No. 9994 or the Expanded Senior Citizens Act of 2010. In this study, it refers to the elderly residents of LGU-Bulan whose profiles and benefit records are managed through the system.

**Web-based System.** A software application that can be accessed through a web browser without requiring any installation on the user's device. In this study, it refers to the system for the Office of Senior Citizen Affairs of LGU-Bulan that allows authorized users to manage senior citizen records and benefits online.

### 4.1 Concept

The concept of the Office of Senior Citizen Affairs Assistant Management System is a centralized web-based platform that efficiently manages and organizes senior citizen records in Bulan, Sorsogon. The system replaces manual processes by providing features for profiling senior citizens, verifying their eligibility, monitoring the distribution of benefits such as pensions and grants, and maintaining accurate transaction records. It also includes descriptive analytics that present summarized data through charts and reports to support decision-making and planning. The system offers role-based access for three roles, the OSCA Admin, the OSCA Head, and the BSCA Presidents, to ensure data security and proper management. This project follows the Rapid Application Development (RAD) methodology, allowing continuous improvement and flexibility throughout the development process to effectively meet the needs of OSCA personnel.

[FIGURE: Insert Fig. 4.1 System Architecture Diagram]

**Fig. 4.1: System Architecture Diagram**

Figure 4.1 shows the architecture of the Office of Senior Citizen Affairs Assistant Management System. The system has three groups of users: the OSCA Head, the BSCA Presidents, and the OSCA Admin. They reach the system over the Internet through a web browser on a desktop computer or on an Android or iOS device. The whole application runs on cloud hosting and is divided into a presentation layer, a business logic layer, a set of modules, and a data layer.

The presentation layer is hosted on Vercel. Every request for data or files is passed to the business logic layer through the /api and /storage paths. The business logic layer is built with Laravel 12 on PHP 8.2. The Notifications module delivers in-app notifications, which are stored in the database, and email advisories, which are sent through Gmail by way of a Google Apps Script relay. The system does not send SMS. Below the modules is the data layer. The MySQL 8.0 database stores all records, and a private file storage keeps senior documents, release proofs, and message attachments, which are served only to users whose role is allowed to see them.

[FIGURE: Insert Fig. 4.2.2 Data Flow Diagram Level 1]

**Fig. 4.2.2: Data Flow Diagram Level 1**

Figure 4.2.2 shows the Level 1 Data Flow Diagram, which breaks the system into seven processes: 1.0 Registration, 2.0 Eligibility Verification, 3.0 Benefit Release, 4.0 Notifications and Messaging, 5.0 User Management, 6.0 Reports and Analytics, and 7.0 Age Threshold Detection. The processes exchange data with three external entities, the BSCA President, the OSCA Head, and the OSCA Admin. They use five data stores: DB1 Senior Citizen Records, DB2 Benefit Distribution Records, DB3 User Data, DB4 Analytics and Reports Data, and DB5 Notifications and Announcements.

**1.0 Registration.** The BSCA President sends the senior's information and documents, together with update and archive requests. The process sends a duplicate check query to DB1 and receives any matching record. If there is no duplicate, it writes the new record to DB1 as pending. It returns a registration confirmation to the President and raises a pending registration event for Process 4.0, which notifies the Admin and the Head that a new registration is waiting for review. A bulk upload sends them one summary notification for the whole file. When a President asks to change a registered record, the process forwards the edit request to the OSCA Head for approval. The Head returns the edit request decision, and approved updates are written to DB1.

**2.0 Eligibility Verification.** The process reads the pending records from DB1. It sends a verification request to both the OSCA Admin and the OSCA Head, and either of them returns an eligibility decision. The updated status (active, inactive, or in review) is written back to DB1, and the registration status is sent to the President. When the documents need to be corrected, the President sends resubmitted documents back to this process.

**3.0 Benefit Release.** The process reads the active seniors per barangay from DB1. The OSCA Head sends the release schedule and the verification of benefit requests. The process writes each release batch and its transactions to DB2. It sends the roster, the schedule, and the request status to the President, who returns the release status of each senior, the proof of release, and new benefit requests. The OSCA Admin sends the batch completion after the release date. A release schedule event is passed to Process 4.0.

**4.0 Notifications and Messaging.** The process gathers events from the other processes: pending registrations from 1.0, release schedules from 3.0, report events from 6.0, and age milestone alerts from 7.0. It reads the user contact information from DB3 and stores the notifications and messages in DB5, from which it reads the unread notifications back. It sends the President email and in-app notifications, advisories, and alerts, and it exchanges messages with the President. It sends alerts and messages to the Head and receives the Head's announcements. It also sends alerts and messages to the Admin and receives the Admin's notification settings.

**5.0 User Management.** The OSCA Admin sends account details and permissions. The process checks the existing accounts in DB3, writes the user updates and an audit entry to DB3, and returns the account list and the audit trail to the Admin.

**6.0 Reports and Analytics.** The OSCA Admin sends a report request, or a revision of a returned report. The process reads the senior data from DB1 and the benefit data from DB2, computes the report figures, and stores them in DB4, from which it reads the stored report data. The draft report goes to the OSCA Head, who returns an approval decision. A returned report goes back to the Admin with the Head's remarks. Once published, the report is sent to the President, and a report event is passed to Process 4.0.

**7.0 Age Threshold Detection.** The process reads the birthdates of the active seniors from DB1 and sends age milestone alerts to Process 4.0, which notifies the users concerned.

### 4.3 Development Model

The study adopted the Rapid Application Development (RAD) model as its software development methodology. RAD is an iterative, user-centered development process which assesses its progress through ongoing stakeholder assessments instead of fixed sequential planning. It suits this system because the OSCA profiling and benefit monitoring system needed regular updates based on user feedback. The RAD model consists of four phases: Requirements Planning, User Design, Rapid Construction, and Cutover (Deployment) [22].

[FIGURE: RAD Methodology]

**Fig. 4.3: RAD Methodology**

**Requirements Planning.** The development team, together with the OSCA Admin staff and the OSCA Head of LGU-Bulan, identified the functional and non-functional system requirements. The project team identified the main requirements, which included senior citizen profiling, eligibility verification, benefit distribution tracking, user role management, descriptive analytics, and report generation. The office's operational needs were documented through interviews and the analysis of existing manual records, which served as the primary data collection methods. This phase established all the development activities which followed it.

**User Design (Prototyping, Iteration, and Workflows).** The User Design phase is the essential element of the RAD model because it consists of three continuous sub-processes: Prototyping, Iteration, and Workflows.

**Prototyping.** The developers built the first operational models, which demonstrated the main functions of the system through three main modules: the senior citizen registration form, the benefit monitoring dashboard, and the analytics view. The OSCA Admin and OSCA Head received the prototypes for evaluation and feedback.

**Iteration.** The prototypes underwent multiple improvements based on stakeholder feedback. Through this iterative process, the team defined age validation during registration, duplicate record checking, the data privacy consent, the return of registrations for document correction, the inactive senior citizen flag, benefit requests, release batches, and the role permissions that the Admin manages. The final design developed an accurate representation of office operations.

**Workflows.** The office workflows were mapped to create a system that operates from senior citizen registration through eligibility verification, benefit release, and reporting according to actual OSCA office procedures. This sub-process was also used to define the permissions which set the system access rights and operational capabilities of the BSCA President, OSCA Admin, and OSCA Head users.

**Rapid Construction.** The developers constructed the full system based on the approved prototypes and finalized workflows. This included all system components: senior citizen registration and profiling, eligibility verification, benefit release batches with proof of release, benefit requests, notifications and email advisories, analytics with age threshold detection, report generation with review, publication, and export, announcements and messages, role permissions, audit logs, and database backup and restore. The design verification from the previous phase enabled the construction to progress smoothly with minimal need for design changes. The developers used web development tools and technologies to build a secure, functional, and accessible system.

**Cutover (Deployment).** The last stage of the project is the deployment of the finished system for the OSCA office of LGU-Bulan. This involves system testing, user acceptance testing (UAT) with designated users, and the migration from the existing manual records to the web-based system. The OSCA Admin, the OSCA Head, and the BSCA Presidents receive training to learn how to operate the system. All testing findings are resolved before the system enters its full operational phase. ⟦CONFIRM: state where the system is hosted, e.g., "The system is deployed online, with the frontend on Vercel and the backend and database on Railway, and is accessed through a web browser."⟧

The RAD model was selected for this study because the system's requirements were closely tied to the specific and evolving needs of the OSCA office. The stakeholders participated in system development through its iterative process, which protected against creating a product that does not fulfill actual user requirements. The study also required a methodology that matched its time limitations, so RAD became the best method to develop the system because it focuses on fast prototyping and quick project completion.

### 4.4 Development Approach

The system used a bottom-up development approach because this method gave an effective solution for working with the Office of Senior Citizen Affairs of LGU-Bulan. The proponents first developed and tested the smaller and basic parts of the system, such as senior citizen record management, the registration forms, data storage, and report generation functions. When the core modules were working, the proponents integrated them into the complete system.

The development happened through several stages, where the OSCA staff tested and reviewed each module and gave feedback for improvement. This continued until all modules were fully integrated and the client was satisfied with the developed system. The bottom-up approach was chosen because it supported a gradual building process, made testing of each component easier, and helped a smoother integration of the system features, based on the structured analysis and design paradigm that the developers followed.

### 4.5 Software Development Tools

The development of a modern web-based system requires a carefully selected set of software tools that support the entire development lifecycle, from writing and structuring code to managing versions and collaborating effectively [23]. The tools used in this study were chosen based on their widespread industry adoption, compatibility with the chosen technology stack, and suitability for building scalable and maintainable web applications.

This section discusses the specific frontend and backend development technologies, as well as the version control system and development environment utilized throughout the project.

#### 4.5.1 Frontend Development

Frontend development refers to the part of web development concerned with what users see and interact with directly in their browsers. It encompasses the design, structure, and behavior of the user interface. For this project, the frontend was built using a combination of core web technologies and a modern JavaScript library to ensure a responsive, dynamic, and user-friendly experience.

**HTML5.** HyperText Markup Language 5 serves as the fundamental structure which defines and organizes the content of the system's pages [24]. The system uses semantic markup to create essential interface components such as the Senior Citizen Registration form, the Eligibility Review page, and the Reports and Analytics dashboard.

**CSS3.** Cascading Style Sheets 3 establishes the visual presentation rules which apply to the structure [25]. It does not define content but instead governs how the HTML elements appear to the user, controlling layout, color schemes, typography, spacing, and responsiveness. CSS3 enables the staff of the municipal office and the BSCA Presidents in the barangays to operate the system on all their devices through media queries and flexible grid layouts which maintain usability and visual consistency.

**JavaScript.** JavaScript enables web pages to provide interactive elements which HTML and CSS do not provide [26]. The system uses JavaScript to perform real-time form validation during senior citizen registration, which blocks users from submitting incomplete or incorrect data before it reaches the server. The system also updates the interface when benefit records change without refreshing the entire page, which creates a smoother and quicker experience for users.

**React and TypeScript.** React is a JavaScript library for building user interfaces from reusable components [37]. The system's pages, such as the senior citizen records, the eligibility review, the benefit release roster, the analytics dashboard, and the reports page, are built as React components written in TypeScript, which adds type checking that catches errors before the code runs. TanStack Router handles navigation between pages and blocks pages that a user's role is not allowed to open, TanStack Query loads and caches the data from the API, Tailwind CSS provides the styling, Recharts draws the charts of the analytics and reports, and SheetJS reads bulk-upload spreadsheets and exports lists to Excel.

#### 4.5.2 Backend Development

Backend development encompasses the server-side logic, database management, and application programming interfaces (APIs) that power the functionality of a web application. It operates behind the scenes, processing requests from the frontend, managing business logic, and retrieving or storing data from the database. For this project, the backend was developed using the Laravel framework and MySQL as the database management system.

**Laravel.** The system's business logic depends on Laravel 12, which functions as its primary framework [27]. Laravel Sanctum secures the API, and Spatie Laravel Permission stores the permissions that the Admin turns on or off for the Head and the BSCA Presidents, enforcing the three-level User Roles and Access Control on registration, eligibility verification, benefit release, and record viewing. Eloquent ORM enables easy management of relational data through its ability to connect a senior citizen's profile with their benefit records, eligibility status, and notification history. Laravel's scheduler checks every day at 12:05 AM for senior citizens who have reached a milestone age and copies new posts from the OSCA Facebook page into the announcements every 15 minutes. Laravel Mail sends email advisories to the BSCA Presidents through Gmail when the Admin turns them on.

**MySQL.** MySQL stores all permanent system data, which includes senior citizen profiles, benefit transaction records, eligibility statuses, reports, notifications, and audit logs [28]. The system's interconnected data requires a relational structure because a single senior citizen record in Bulan connects to multiple benefit releases, eligibility decisions, and notifications which depend on continuous and reliable relational integrity.
